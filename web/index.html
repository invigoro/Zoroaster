How the prophecy is made

Every night just after midnight UTC, Zoroaster reads two things, both as they stood at the end of the day before: the English Wikipedia pages its first model expects to see a burst of editing, and the past week's stories on Wikipedia's Portal:Current events. A language model running on the prophet's own machine asks what each page or story brings next within the week, and writes a prediction.

Separate checks drop any prediction that names a person, or a specific organization in a war, a disaster or a crime; some are rewritten in general terms instead. They also drop predictions that restate what was already known, and send back any that add a number or a name their evidence doesn't give. At most ten a day are published, world events first, and sport only when it settles a title.
